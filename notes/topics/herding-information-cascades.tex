\chapter{Herding and Information Cascades}

\begin{itemize}
    \item People change their behavior based on what others are doing
    \item Information cascade: people make same decisions as others regardless of private information
    \item Subjects use conditional probability to make the best decisions based on others' behaviors
    \item Bayes' Rule:
          \begin{equation}
              \prob(A \given B) = \frac{\prob(A) \prob(B \given A)}{\prob(B)}
          \end{equation}
\end{itemize}

\section{A General Cascade Model}

\begin{itemize}
    \item People decide sequentially whether to accept or reject; accepting is good with probability \(p\)
    \item Each person receives an informative private signal, but sees only earlier decisions
    \item If acceptances \(A\) and rejections \(B\) differ by at least two, follow the majority regardless of the private signal
    \item In the cupcake experiment, two matching early guesses can start a cascade; a wrong cascade occurs with probability \(1/9\)
    \item A cascade can be wrong even when everyone acts rationally; a few people with better information or different actions can break it
\end{itemize}
