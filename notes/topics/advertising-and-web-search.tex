\chapter{Advertising and Web Search}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Search ads are unique in that they serve targeted ads with specific interests at the \emph{right time}
    \item Determine prices for ads with \emph{auctions}
    \item Value of an ad slot: click-through rate, revenue per click, etc.
    \item We could make market clearing prices if we know advertisers' valuations, but we generally don't
    \item 1st-price auction causes underreporting of value, and 2nd-price auction is only tested on single-item auctions
    \item Incorporate idea of ``harm'': does a bidder cause harm to other bidders by winning a certain slot?
    \item Need to compare the total valuation of other bidders to the total valuation if the bidder were to leave the auction
    \item Difference in total valuations is the value gain if the bidder were to leave the auction
    \item The winner of the least-valuable slot does not make any effect
    \item Differences in total valuations are the Vickrey-Clarke-Groves (VCG) prices
\end{itemize}

\section{VCG Auctions}

\subsection{Process}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Buyers submit bids for each item
    \item Choose socially-optimal matching (e.g.\ with market-clearing prices)
    \item Charge each buyer the VCG price for their winning item
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Properties}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Bidding your true value is the dominant strategy
    \item Maximizes total valuation of the perfect matching from slots to advertisers
\end{itemize}

\section{Real-World Pricing}

\begin{itemize}
    \item Generalized second-price auction
    \item Buyers submit bids
    \item Sell each slot $i$ to the $i$th highest bidder, at the $i + 1$st highest bid
    \item But, bidding true value is \emph{not} dominant strategy
\end{itemize}
